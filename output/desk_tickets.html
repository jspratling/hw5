Reflection

Based on the real run on 2026-11-06 (tickets 101, 102, 103). Evidence is on the ticket tabs and the Cash tab.

1. How I would grade the agents on each ticket

I grade each ticket from 1 to 5. Every ticket starts at 5 and loses 1 point for each problem area: accuracy, returns, customer complaints, and sales that don't reach a 61% margin.

Ticket 101 (Tauhid's tee)4 of 5. Every number was right: tee size S at 0, invoice 501 $840 and 3 days overdue, $3,400 → $2,560 after David approved. No returns or complaints, and the tee's margin is 71.4% ($28 vs. $8 cost). One point off for accuracy: the first final check left the ticket open "pending tee arrival," and it took a second check to resolve.
Ticket 102 (rent)5 of 5. Facilities found the right rent ($2,400, due Sept 2), held it as I decided, prepared no payment, and drafted one landlord note that names no fee amount (the lease has no fee terms). No sale, so margin doesn't apply; no complaints.
Ticket 103 (Yale AI Club)4 of 5. Shipped 8, ordered 17, billed 20 on 2% net 10 ($1,160, or $1,136.80 early), and David approved the $374 restock, ending at $2,186. The margin with the discount is 61.3%, just above my 61% line. One point off for accuracy: Customer Service never told the club that 8 ship now and 12 arrive about Sept 5, and the Boss asked Accounting twice for the same work.

2. Actual compared with my Expected plan

  • 101: The money, tools and outcome matched my plan exactly. The routing didn't: I expected the Boss to call Accounting first, but he called Inventory first because he wanted stock and the lead time before anything else. The handoff then ran Inventory → Accounting instead of Accounting → Inventory. Customer Service drafted Tauhid's note as planned, plus an updated one after the payment.
  • 102: Closest to my plan. Facilities went first and the rent was held with a landlord note, as planned. The difference: I expected Facilities to ask Accounting about the cash, but it checked the cash itself, so there was no handoff.
  • 103: The shipping, order, bill, margin and money matched. Again the Boss called Inventory first, not Accounting, and the handoff ran Inventory → Accounting. Customer Service was never used, so the club's "8 now, 12 later" message I planned is missing. The Boss also made one extra request to Accounting.

Overall, the agents got the money and the end results right on all three tickets, but they routed the work their own way. Inventory first actually makes sense for orders, because you need to know what's on the shelf before you bill.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

One agent is simpler because it cuts out the routing between agents and makes flaws in the system easier to pinpoint. Ticket 102 proves it: only Facilities did any work, using four lookups and one draft (ticket, rent status, approvals, cash, save draft). One agent with those tools would have done the same job without the Boss handing it off, for fewer model calls (this ticket used 10 calls and about 27,000 tokens). The extra handoffs on 101 and 103 are also where the flaws showed up: the ticket left open and the missed club message. With one agent, there'd be one place to look. The team only earned its keep on 103, where stock, an order, a bill and a payment all had to happen in the right order.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools it has

  1. Another vendor bill comes due. Accounting finds it with list_open_invoices, checks cash with get_cash_balance, and prepares it with prepare_payment for David to approve.
  2. Another size or product runs out (for example the Crest Mug, already at 0). Inventory checks it with check_stock, picks the vendor and arrival date with get_vendors (Elm City Gifts, 3 days), and orders with place_restock_order.
  3. A customer asks when their order arrives or what it costs. Customer Service uses check_stock, get_vendors and quote_order, and drafts the answer with save_draft for the Boss to approve.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

  1. A customer returns an item. There's no returns table or tool to put stock back or refund money. It needs a record_return tool, stock that can go up, and a refund payment that David approves.
  2. A customer actually pays (the Yale AI Club's $1,136.80). Cash can only go out; there's no receivables table or way to record money coming in. It needs customer invoices, a record_customer_payment tool, and Accounting tracking who still owes the shop. That would also let the rent be paid.
  3. Sending the messages for real and handling complaints. Drafts never leave the board, and there's no complaints log. It needs an email tool (with David approving each send) and a complaints table, plus a Customer Service step that follows up. That way the complaint part of my grading rubric could actually be measured.

In the end, the agents handle the shop's administrative and operations work: stock, bills, rent and customer drafts. A person, David, still makes every money decision.
